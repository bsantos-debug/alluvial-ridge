\subsection{Archived first-flood validation}
\label{sec:first-flood-validation}
The corrected rebuild 002 provides provisional evidence over the first
20-day hydrograph. These archived observations establish a limited
numerical check; they do not supply any requested manuscript panel.
The 250 m and 500 m floodplain cases use configuration 11 and the full
72 km by 48 km domain. Their recorded parameters differ only in
floodplain spacing; channel-node spacing remains 250 m. The capture
verified both completed process records and their output hashes.

The coarser grid captures 49 bank cells after the first hydrograph,
whereas the finer grid captures none. This difference changes the
reservoir available for later recycling. Capture occurs after the
hydrograph and therefore cannot explain the first-flood deposit-mass
difference. The adaptive time steps also change with floodplain
spacing. Two first-flood cases with fixed channel spacing do not
establish spatial or temporal convergence, nor do they test recycling
through a subsequent complete hydrograph.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to diagnostic definitions.}
The published description does not fix all spatial averages in
$\beta$. Reprocessing the same archived day-20 fields gives:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Nominal central reach & $\beta$, 250 m & Change (\%) & $\beta$, 500 m & Change (\%) \\
\midrule
60\% & $1.287\times10^{-3}$ & -21.73 & $3.426\times10^{-3}$ & -18.02 \\
80\% & $1.644\times10^{-3}$ & 0.00 & $4.178\times10^{-3}$ & 0.00 \\
100\% & $2.852\times10^{-3}$ & 73.52 & $5.602\times10^{-3}$ & 34.07 \\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
Only the reach used for the transverse relief profile changes in this
table. The distal bands remain at nominal 10\% of the domain width at each side,
and the denominator remains the full-interface mean bankfull depth.
Changes are relative to the raster reconstruction of the default
central-80\% estimator. Integer trimming gives central lengths of
43.5 and 44 km at nominal 60\%, respectively; both grids use 58 km
at nominal 80\% and 72 km at 100\%. Their default distal bands are
4.75 and 4.5 km wide on each side.
Thus including the end regions materially changes first-flood ridge
relief. This comparison identifies sensitivity to the averaging
definition; it does not isolate a boundary-condition mechanism.

At the default reach, changing each distal band to 5\%, 10\%, or 20\%
changes $\beta$ by at most 0.24\%.
Holding the default ridge relief fixed and restricting the channel-depth
average to bank faces in the nominal central 60\%, 80\%, or full reach
changes $\beta$ by at most 0.20\%.
The source record retains all nine reach/band combinations and the
three separate denominator variants.

For day-12 $\alpha$, thresholds of 0.1, 1, and 10 mm leave every
sampled bank face wet in both cases. The full-domain mean depth,
including dry cells, is unchanged, so $\alpha$ is unchanged.
The nominal wet floodplain area nevertheless varies with the threshold:
3389.625--3404.875 km$^2$ at 250 m and 3371.250--3406.750 km$^2$ at 500 m.
No tested wetness classification is ambiguous within float32 rounding.

The default reconstructions of ridge relief, channel depth,
$\beta$, and $\alpha$ agree with the double-precision summaries
within propagated half-ULP snapshot bounds and the recorded arithmetic
and CSV rounding allowances. These bounds describe representation
precision, not discretization or physical uncertainty. The scenarios
do not select a preferred estimator and do not establish robustness
of the late 20-flood or 200-flood manuscript statistics.
\path{figures/first_flood_diagnostic_sensitivity.json} records the
definitions, effective grid windows, bounds, and input identities.

\subsection{Completed first-flood time-step sensitivity}
A later completed case reduces the CFL limit from 0.4 to 0.2 while
holding the corrected executable, configuration 11, 250 m floodplain
grid, and 250 m initial channel spacing fixed. Both calculations
complete the full 20-day hydrograph with exit code zero. The retained
archive contains both original process records and all recorded
outputs. Macrosteps increase from 125,765 to 250,265 and channel substeps from
452,282 to 899,949; adaptive steps are not uniformly halved at every time.

End-flood ridge height (m) & $1.31874\times10^{-2}$ & $1.32257\times10^{-2}$ & +0.28975 \\

Percentage changes use the CFL 0.4 value as denominator. Connectivity
is sampled on day 12; ridge height, relative relief, channel depth,

Deposited mineral mass is the accumulated source ledger retained in
the post-remap checkpoint, not an integral of the remaining deposit.
Both inundated interface fractions equal one, so the connectivity
change is the change in mean floodplain depth.

The end-flood masks agree. Over all 3420 km$^2$ of floodplain,
including dry cells, water-depth differences have mean absolute value
0.00941692 m and maximum 0.06339424 m. Event-deposit thickness differences have
mean absolute value 0.00003672 m and maximum 0.00362724 m.
The respective bounds from float32 storage on these mean differences
are $9.25765\times10^{-9}$ m and $3.50178\times10^{-11}$ m; they exclude discretization and separate
floating-point arithmetic error.

The half-CFL post-remap residuals are $-1.71661\times10^{-5}$ m$^3$ water,
$-1.46355\times10^{0}$ kg suspension plus deposition, and $-6.92458\times10^{-1}$ kg total solids.
Normalized by prescribed upstream inputs of $1.512\times10^{10}$ m$^3$ and
$5.47346\times10^{10}$ kg, their magnitudes are below $2.7\times10^{-11}$.
These denominators exclude recycling, outflow, and signed bed forcing.
Both first remaps have zero captured or abandoned cells, bank reservoir,
recycled mass, cutoffs, and water or mass corrections. Signed bed
forcing is retained as a separate ledger in the comparison.

The distinct sensitivities of water storage and ridge height require
both hydraulic and deposition quantities to be assessed. Two CFL
limits do not determine a temporal convergence order or establish
time-step independence. This first-flood comparison does not test
later recycling, 580-flood evolution, or manuscript-panel agreement.

\subsection{Completed first-flood deposition-threshold sensitivity}
This comparison reduces the assumed critical deposition stress $\tau_c$
from 0.1 to 0.05 Pa. The silt and clay settling rates contain the factor
$\max(0,1-\tau_b/\tau_c)$; lowering $\tau_c$ suppresses their settling
at fixed bed shear stress and concentration. Sand settling has no
shear reduction. Both cases use the corrected executable, configuration
11, 250 m floodplain grid, 250 m initial channel spacing, CFL limit
0.4, and one complete 20-day hydrograph. All other numerical parameters
agree. Both recorded processes exit successfully.

\midrule

End-flood ridge height (m) & $1.31874\times10^{-2}$ & $1.41532\times10^{-2}$ & +7.32334 \\

Deposited mineral mass (kg) & $2.24279\times10^{9}$ & $1.80697\times10^{9}$ & -19.43183 \\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
Percentage changes use the 0.1 Pa case as denominator. Connectivity
is sampled on day 12; ridge height, relative relief, channel depth,
and water storage are sampled on day 20 before migration/remapping.
Deposited mineral mass is the accumulated deposition-source ledger
in the checkpoint. It differs from the remaining event-deposit volume,
which includes pore space. Both day-12 inundated interface fractions
equal one, so the connectivity difference is a mean-depth difference.

The ridge-height metric increases by 7.323\% while total deposited
mineral mass decreases by 19.432\%. Ridge relief and integrated
deposition therefore respond differently in this comparison. The
local settling factor alone does not determine the sign of the
coupled spatial response.

The end-flood masks agree. Across all 3420 km$^2$ of floodplain,
including dry cells, water-depth differences have mean absolute value
0.00009540 m and maximum 0.00367419 m. Event-deposit thickness differences have
mean absolute value 0.00011204 m and maximum 0.00822584 m.
The respective float32 storage bounds on the mean differences are
$9.58587\times10^{-9}$ m and $3.28866\times10^{-11}$ m. They exclude discretization, separate floating-point
arithmetic error, and rounding of the tabulated values.

The 0.05 Pa post-remap residuals are $-5.13196\times10^{-5}$ m$^3$ water,
$-6.3638\times10^{-1}$ kg suspension plus deposition, and $-3.44117\times10^{-1}$ kg total solids.
The largest residual magnitude normalized by the corresponding
prescribed upstream input is $1.16266\times10^{-11}$; the denominators are $1.512\times10^{10}$ m$^3$
and $5.47346\times10^{10}$ kg. They exclude recycling, outflow, and signed bed forcing.
Both first remaps have zero captured or abandoned cells, bank mass,
recycled mass, cutoffs, and correction terms. Signed bed forcing
remains a separate ledger in the retained comparison.

This result quantifies sensitivity to an assumed deposition parameter.
It does not constrain the appropriate physical threshold, numerical
convergence, later bank recycling, 580-flood behavior, or agreement
with a manuscript panel.
